The atom, and how small it is

Keep cutting a piece of wood in half. What is the smallest piece you could possibly end up with?

Cutting wood down

Wood is made up of different substances. The most abundant compound in it is cellulose, and cellulose is made of the elements carbon, hydrogen and oxygen, chemically combined. Each of those elements consists of atoms.

Definition An atom is the simplest unit of an element.

The word comes from the Greek atomos, meaning “cannot be cut” or “indivisible”.

Each element in the Periodic Table consists of only one type of atom. The element carbon, which partly makes up the cellulose in wood, consists of only carbon atoms.

How small is an atom?

An atom cannot be seen with the naked eye. The only way to get a feel for the scale is to count how many would fit along something familiar.

Zooming in

Working at that scale: nanotechnology

Nanoscale objects are tiny objects with sizes of less than 0.000 000 001 metre — one billionth of a metre. A strand of human DNA is about 0.000 000 002 5 m in diameter, so it is a nanoscale object.

Ordinary microscopes cannot see things that small. Scientists use special instruments — the atomic force microscope and the scanning tunnelling microscope.

Nanotechnology The field of science that involves nanoscale objects and their applications. One application is very small robots called nanorobots, which can deliver drugs into the bloodstream and repair damaged cells.
Why the chapter starts here Everything that follows — the sub-atomic particles, isotopes, molecules, nanorobots — depends on accepting that matter is built from units far too small to see. Scientists could only work that out once the technology existed to find the evidence. That is why the chapter opens by saying people once believed matter was made of water, wood, air, fire, earth and metal: the model changed because the evidence changed.
