Does rust need water and air?

The classic investigation, side by side: three controlled reference tubes, plus one you control yourself.

How to read this

Each tube holds an identical iron nail. What differs is what each nail is exposed to. Drag the day slider — all four tubes age together — and watch which ones actually rust.

Day 7

All four nails age together. Only the ones with both water and air present will actually rust — that's the whole conclusion of this experiment, right there in front of you.

Why each tube is set up this way

  • Tube A is the "everything present" case — the control that shows rusting can happen at all.
  • Tube B keeps water but removes air: boiling water drives out dissolved oxygen, and a floating layer of oil stops more air from dissolving back in.
  • Tube C keeps air but removes water: a drying agent (anhydrous calcium chloride) soaks up any moisture before it can reach the nail.
  • Tube D is yours — try every combination and confirm the rule for yourself: rust needs water and oxygen, together, or it doesn't form at all.
