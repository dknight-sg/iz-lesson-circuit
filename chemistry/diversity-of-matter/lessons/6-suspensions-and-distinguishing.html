Suspensions, and telling everything apart

Two tests that separate a solution from a suspension, then the three questions that separate everything from everything else.

Suspensions

Definition A suspension is a mixture that contains insoluble substances in a solvent.

Dirty water from a drain is the textbook example — it contains substances that do not dissolve in water. Wastewater, calamine lotion and some soups are suspensions too. Because the substances are insoluble, three things follow, and each one is a test you can do:

The insoluble substances in a suspension can be removed by filtration — Chapter 4. You cannot filter a solution, because the dissolved particles are as small as the water particles and go straight through the paper.

Element, compound or mixture? Three questions

This is Table 3.3, and it is the most examinable thing in the chapter. An element is made of only one type of substance, so the three questions do not apply to it. For everything else, ask:

Notice the pattern The compound column reads yes · no · no and the mixture column reads no · yes · yes. They are exact opposites on every line. If you remember either column, you have the other for free.

Is smoke an element, a compound or a mixture? A mixture. Evidence: it contains several gases (carbon dioxide, water vapour, others) plus tiny solid particles; its composition varies with what is burning; the solid particles can be separated from the gases by filtering.
