Putting it together

Chemical changes in daily life

The same rearranging-atoms idea shows up everywhere — sometimes it helps you, sometimes it costs you.

Quick recap

  • A chemical change forms a genuinely new substance — atoms rearrange, but nothing is created or destroyed, so mass is conserved.
  • You describe one with a word equation: reactants → products.
  • The families to recognise: combustion, thermal decomposition, oxidation (including rusting), and reactions between acids and alkalis, metals, or carbonates.
  • Indicators reveal whether something is acidic, neutral, or alkaline by colour.

The same chemistry, cutting both ways

None of these reactions are "good" or "bad" by nature — it depends entirely on whether you wanted them to happen.

Working for us

Cooking makes food safe and digestible. Respiration powers every cell in your body. Electroplating adds a protective, decorative metal layer to jewellery and cutlery. Medicines are manufactured through carefully controlled reactions.

Working against us

Rusting weakens bridges and cars. Spoilage wastes food and can make you ill. Uncontrolled fires destroy property and life. Burning fuel releases gases that pollute the air we breathe.
