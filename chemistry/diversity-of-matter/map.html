The three definitions

ElementThe basic building blocks of living and non-living matter. Cannot be broken down into simpler substances. One kind of atom. 118 known; arranged by properties in the Periodic Table; metals (most) and non-metals.
CompoundTwo or more elements chemically combined. Formed by a chemical reaction. Fixed proportion by mass. Properties different from its elements. Not easily separated — needs a lot of energy.
MixtureTwo or more elements and/or compounds not chemically combined. Formed by a physical change. Proportion not fixed. Properties of constituents retained. Easily separated by physical means.

Air

Nitrogen 78% · oxygen 21% · carbon dioxide, water vapour and other gases 1%.

  • Nitrogen and oxygen: usually constant over time and place.
  • Carbon dioxide, water vapour and dust: vary with time and place.
  • High altitude: same composition, less air — so less oxygen per breath.
  • Singapore measures air quality with the PSI (six pollutants). Under 100: normal activity. Over 100: heart/lung patients avoid strenuous outdoor exercise.

Solutions

  • Solution — a mixture in which one substance dissolves completely in another. Solute dissolves; solvent does the dissolving. Water: the universal solvent.
  • Dilute — low percentage of solute in a fixed volume. Concentrated — high percentage.
  • Soluble — dissolves completely. Insoluble — does not dissolve.

Solubility — definitionThe largest amount of solute that can dissolve in a fixed volume of a particular solvent at a specific temperature.

A suspension is a mixture containing insoluble substances in a solvent — dirty water, wastewater, calamine lotion, some soups. Insoluble substances are removed by filtration.

3.1 — why it matters

  • Melamine: useful in plastics; harmful in food. Mixed into milk powder in 2008 because its composition resembles milk protein, fooling protein tests. Babies suffered kidney damage.
  • Recycling needs you to know what an item is made of. E-waste contains hazardous metals and other substances — separate bin, never general rubbish.
