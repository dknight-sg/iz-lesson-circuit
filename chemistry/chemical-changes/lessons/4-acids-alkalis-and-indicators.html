Acids, alkalis and indicators

You can't see acidity. Indicators turn it into a colour you can.

The pH scale

Every solution sits somewhere on the pH scale, from 1 to 14:

  • pH below 7 — acidic. The lower the number, the more strongly acidic. Vinegar, lemon juice, dilute hydrochloric acid.
  • pH exactly 7 — neutral. Pure water.
  • pH above 7 — alkaline. The higher the number, the more strongly alkaline. Soap, baking soda solution, dilute sodium hydroxide.

Reading the scale with indicators

An indicator is a chemical that changes colour depending on whether it's in an acid, a neutral solution, or an alkali. You'll meet three kinds:

  • Litmus paper — the simplest. Red litmus turns blue in an alkali; blue litmus turns red in an acid. Neither changes in a neutral solution. It tells you acid-or-alkali, but not how strong.
  • Universal Indicator (UI) — a full spectrum from red (strongly acidic) through green (neutral) to purple (strongly alkaline), matched against a colour chart to read off an approximate pH number.
  • Natural indicators — some plant pigments do the same job. Red cabbage juice, for instance, is pink-red in acid and blue-green in alkali.

Quick check

Q A solution turns red litmus paper blue. Is the solution acidic, neutral, or alkaline?

Red litmus only turns blue in an alkali — so the solution is alkaline.
