Five techniques, and one decision that picks between them. Seven lessons, seven labs, a chapter map, and thirty questions.

The one idea behind the whole chapter

Chapter 3 told you that the constituents of a mixture keep their own physical properties. This chapter turns that into a tool: if two substances differ in some property, that difference is a handle you can grab to pull them apart.

Every question in this chapter is really this question Which property is different? — and then the technique follows automatically.

Magnetic or not → magnet. Different particle sizes → filtration. A dissolved solid you want → evaporation. A liquid you want → distillation. Different solubilities → chromatography.

You will only ever be asked to memorise five techniques. What earns the marks is choosing the right one, in the right order, and saying why.

This is scoped for G3. The reverse-osmosis detail the textbook marks optional for G2 is core here — and it is exactly the part Section 4.3 is built on.
