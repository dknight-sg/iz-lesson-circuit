Compounds

Two gases combine and you get a liquid. That is what “chemically combined” means — and it is the whole difference between a compound and a mixture.

What a compound is

Definition Compounds consist of two or more elements that are chemically combined together.

Water is the compound you meet most often: hydrogen and oxygen combined in a chemical reaction. Carbon dioxide is another — carbon and oxygen. It is in the air, plants need it for photosynthesis, and because it traps heat it is a greenhouse gas; too much of it, from human activity, is warming the Earth.

Three properties every compound has

1 · Its properties are different from its elements'

Hydrogen and oxygen are both gases. Chemically combined, they make water — a liquid. Carbon is a black solid, oxygen a gas; combined, they make carbon dioxide, a colourless gas.

hydrogen (gas) + oxygen (gas) → water (liquid)
carbon (solid) + oxygen (gas) → carbon dioxide (gas)

The most dramatic example is salt: sodium is a metal that fizzes violently in water, and chlorine is a poisonous green gas. Chemically combined, they are the white crystals you put on food.

2 · Its elements are combined in a fixed proportion by mass

Every sample of water, anywhere, has the same ratio of hydrogen to oxygen. You cannot make “water with a bit more oxygen in it” — if the ratio changes, it is a different compound (or no longer a compound at all). This is the property that most clearly separates a compound from a mixture.

3 · It cannot be separated into its elements easily

Once hydrogen and oxygen are combined as water, pulling them apart takes a lot of energy — you have to pass an electric current through the water (electrolysis). Compare a mixture, where you can often just filter or evaporate.

Reversible does not mean easy Water can be broken back into hydrogen and oxygen, with enough energy. But not every chemical reaction can be reversed — once paper has burnt, no amount of energy gives you the paper back. “Hard to separate” is about the compound; “reversible” is about the reaction. Do not confuse them.
