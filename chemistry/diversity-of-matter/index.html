Chapter 3

Diversity of Matter by its Chemical Composition

Everything around you is one of three things — an element, a compound, or a mixture. Six lessons, six labs, one chapter map, and thirty questions.

The one idea behind the whole chapter

The chapter asks four questions in turn — why composition matters, what elements and compounds are, what mixtures are, and how to tell all three apart. But every one of them comes back to a single sorting decision:

Ask this of any substance Is it one kind of atom? → element.
Two or more kinds, chemically joined in a fixed ratio? → compound.
Two or more substances just sharing space, in any ratio? → mixture.

Get that decision right and the rest — solutions, suspensions, the air, steel, seawater — are just examples of the third box.

This is scoped for G3. The textbook marks Solubility as optional for G2; for you it is core, and it is the part most likely to be examined precisely because it is the part people skip.
