Atomic technologies and their issues

Understanding atoms let us image the inside of a body and generate electricity without oil. The same understanding built the atomic bomb.

Making new materials

Scientists constantly seek to find out more about atoms as they try to create new materials from them. Gold-plated orchids exist in Singapore today because a local biochemist managed to coat living plant material with gold atoms.

Medicine

The knowledge of atoms has been used for medical purposes and to provide nuclear energy. In medicine, the properties of atoms are applied in:

  • Radiation therapy, to cure diseases.
  • Medical imaging — magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) and X-ray.

A radiographer is a healthcare professional who uses medical radiation equipment to produce images of the human body, helping doctors diagnose illness or injury. Radiation from the X-ray machine is able to penetrate the skin and bones, and such radiation is given off by atoms that have large amounts of energy.

Nuclear energy

How a nuclear power plant works Nuclear energy comes from the controlled release of heat from radioactive elements such as uranium and plutonium. That heat changes water to steam, which moves turbines to generate electricity.

It is an alternative to using oil, especially in places that have no access to oil. Scientists are now trying to build smaller and more efficient plants.

The sentence the chapter is really built around Social and ethical issues often arise from scientific discoveries. Resolving these issues requires a good understanding of scientific applications and sound moral values from everyone. We need to actively understand and debate these issues in order to make wise decisions that benefit mankind.

If a question asks what should be done about a technology like this, that is the shape of the answer it wants — not “ban it” or “it's fine”, but understand it, debate it, decide wisely.
